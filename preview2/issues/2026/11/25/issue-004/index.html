Mombasa Tea Fetches Its Best Price of 2026 as Kenya Pushes Its Own Brands

Sale 47 averaged $2.39 a kilo, the highest this year on provisional figures, while EATTA and KEPROBA sign a five-year deal to sell less tea in bulk and Uganda puts Shs198 billion on the table for its factories.

By the Chai Weekly desk · 11 min read

NAIROBI — Tea at Mombasa fetched its best average price of the year on Monday. Buyers paid US$2.39 a kilo for 7.81 million kg at Sale 47, up 5 cents on the week, according to EATTA’s provisional figures. The rise comes as the East Africa Tea Trade Association and the Kenya Export Promotion and Branding Agency sign a five-year deal to sell more Kenyan tea in packets under its own name, and less of it in bulk sacks.

The auction this week

The average price at Sale 47 on 23 November was US$2.39 a kilo, 5 cents above the final figure for Sale 46, according to EATTA. Buyers took 7.81 million kg, up about 3% from 7.58 million kg the week before. No orthodox tea was sold at this sale, so the CTC average is also $2.39. These are provisional figures; EATTA finalises them on Friday after late sales.

  • Best of 2026 so far: $2.39 is the highest average of the 47 sales held this year, EATTA’s figures show. The year’s average stands at $2.21 a kilo, and the low was $2.09 in May.
  • Four weeks of strength: the average has stayed between $2.33 and $2.39 since Sale 44 on 2 November, while weekly volumes have risen from 6.66 million kg to 7.81 million kg.
  • Last week’s sale, final: after late sales, Sale 46 ended at US$2.34 a kilo on 7.58 million kg, with orthodox tea at $3.43. Last week’s issue gave the provisional US$2.35, 7.51 million kg and $3.45.

Why it matters: prices are firming even as more tea comes to the floor with the short rains. We could not find a broker’s report on Sale 47 by the time of writing, so we cannot yet say which buyers drove the rise. Every sale is on our Auction analysis page.

KTDA and factory news

A deal to sell Kenyan tea by its name. EATTA and the Kenya Export Promotion and Branding Agency (KEPROBA) have signed a five-year memorandum of understanding to move Kenya’s tea exports from bulk towards packaged, branded and premium products, Sacco Review reported on 19 November. Bulk black tea makes up 96.3% of Kenya’s tea exports. The two bodies plan joint trade missions, market intelligence and exporter training, and will promote a “Kenya Tea Story” under the Made in Kenya brand, including specialty, orthodox, green and purple teas. KEPROBA Acting Chief Executive Officer Mr Austin Macheso said the deal would push exporters beyond bulk trading. EATTA Managing Director Mr George Omuga called for more investment in productivity, branding and new markets.

Brokers told to build their own brands. At a meeting at EATTA the same week, Tea Board of Kenya Chairman Mr Ndung’u Gathinji and Chief Executive Officer Mr Willy Mutai urged tea brokers to invest in packaging and product development and to reach consumers directly, Sacco Review reported. The Board highlighted a planned office in Guangzhou, China, to reach Chinese buyers.

Sh3.5 billion for bonuses, says the Deputy President. Deputy President Kithure Kindiki said in Belgut, Kericho County, on 19 November that the government is speeding up help for tea farmers. “The President has assured farmers that the government will provide 3.5 billion shillings to address the outstanding issues,” he said, referring to a pledge by President William Ruto, Nairobi News and Capital FM reported. Kericho Senator Aaron Cheruiyot thanked the government for the allocation. Neither report said when or how the money will reach farmers.

Mt Kenya growers want the Tea Act enforced. Farmers from Murang’a, Kiambu, Embu, Kirinyaga, Meru and Nyeri met at Kenol Market in Murang’a under the Mt Kenya Farmers Caucus and called for full enforcement of the Tea Act, warning that the gains made since 2020 could be lost, Sacco Review reported on 23 November. Caucus Chairman Mr Johnson Maina proposed an age limit for factory directors, and Mr Isaac Kariuki, a former vice chairman of Thumaita Tea Factory, proposed cutting factory boards from six directors to five. The growers welcomed the court’s suspension of the Sh2.28-a-kilo tea levy.

The KTDA chair case. Last week we reported that the High Court would hear the dispute over the KTDA Holdings chairmanship on 24 November. We have not yet seen a report of what happened at that hearing, and will follow it up next week.

What it means for farmers: branding will not lift this year’s bonus, but more tea sold in packets abroad means more of the shelf price stays in Kenya. Ask your factory’s directors how much of your tea goes out as anything other than bulk.

Sources: Sacco Review, 19 Nov: KEPROBA and EATTA · Sacco Review, 19 Nov: brokers · Nairobi News, 20 Nov · Capital FM, 19 Nov · Sacco Review, 23 Nov: Tea Act

East Africa

Uganda: Shs198 billion to revive the tea factories. Uganda’s government plans to give tea factories Shs152 billion in working capital, once an independent assessment of their debts is done, and to set up a Shs46 billion fertiliser revolving fund, Minister for Finance, Planning and Economic Development Hon. Henry Musasizi told Parliament on 17 November, according to a Parliament of Uganda release. The Uganda Revenue Authority found that tea factories owe Shs39.329 billion in domestic taxes, and Hon. Musasizi said he had asked the URA Commissioner General to review them. The government has paid Shs89.26 billion to verified nursery bed operators and still owes Shs18.9 billion to operators in Buhweju. Hon. Cohen Amanya, MP for Igara County West, who is also behind the proposed National Tea Bill we covered last week, raised the collapse of the sector and asked for tax relief. “The tea sector has the potential to bring a lot of money into the economy,” he said.

Why it matters for Kenya: Uganda sells most of its tea through Mombasa. Working capital and fertiliser could mean more and better Ugandan tea on the Mombasa floor, competing with Kenya’s plainer teas.

We found no new tea reports from Rwanda, Tanzania, Burundi or Malawi this week that we could confirm.

Source: Parliament of Uganda release via African Newspage, 18 Nov

Around the tea world

Sri Lanka: exports dip, Turkey buys more. Sri Lanka earned about $1.08 billion from tea exports in the first nine months of 2026, down from $1.16 billion a year earlier, as volumes fell 4% to 189.4 million kg, the Financial Chronicle reported from Siyaka Research’s analysis of customs data. Turkey more than doubled its purchases, up 134% to 35.7 million kg, while Iraq cut back to 18.4 million kg from 30 million kg. Value-added teas made up about 58% of Sri Lanka’s exports.

Colombo: smaller sales ahead. Colombo’s Sale 45 on 16 and 17 November offered 5.60 million kg and met “fair” demand overall, Forbes & Walker reported, with the best Western teas up Rs20 a kilo or more. Sale 46 this week offered 5.28 million kg and Sale 47 on 1 and 2 December will offer 5.11 million kg, according to the Tea Exporters Association.

Why it matters for Kenya: Sri Lanka sells more than half its tea as value-added products, the road Kenya’s new branding deal points to. Kenya still ships 96.3% of its tea in bulk.

Sources: Financial Chronicle, 20 Oct · Forbes & Walker, 16 & 17 Nov · Tea Exporters Association Sri Lanka

Weather in the tea counties

Kenya Met’s weekly outlook for 24 to 30 November expects rain over parts of Kericho, Nandi, Kisii, Nyamira, Nyeri, Murang’a, Kiambu, Kirinyaga, Embu, Meru and Tharaka Nithi, K24 Digital and The Star reported. Bomet is not on the list. Nights are expected to fall below 10°C in parts of Meru, Tharaka Nithi, Nyeri, Kirinyaga and Murang’a.

Kenya Met expects above-average short rains in more than 40 counties this season and has urged people in flood-prone areas to stay alert. Do not cross flooded roads or streams, and keep away from trees during thunderstorms.

Shamba tip

Replanting? Ask about the new clones. On 17 November, 160 growers from Kiegoi tea factory visited the KALRO Tea Research Institute to learn about elite cultivars, nursery management, uprooting and replanting, and integrated pest and disease management, Sacco Review reported.

  1. Before you uproot old bushes, ask your factory’s extension officer which clones the institute recommends for your altitude.
  2. Raise or buy seedlings early so they are ready to plant while the soil is still wet.
  3. Ask about pest control as a package, not a single spray: the institute teaches integrated pest and disease management.

Your extension officer can arrange a visit for your buying centre too.

Source: Sacco Review, 19 Nov

Factory spotlight

Browns Plantations Kenya. Browns grows tea across Kericho and Bomet counties on more than 5,000 hectares, with four tea factories, nine estates and a sawmill, Food Business MEA reported on 5 November. The company is betting on orthodox tea, the leafy style that sells at a premium: at Mombasa’s Sale 46, orthodox tea averaged $3.43 a kilo against $2.33 for CTC, according to EATTA. A new hybrid line at its Changana factory, making both CTC and orthodox tea, adds about 3 million kg of made tea a year, giving Changana and Kitumbe a combined orthodox capacity of 6 million kg. A third orthodox line, at Kimugu, is due to be commissioned this month with about 3 million kg of capacity and a new garden mark, “Netai” (“The First”). Its Milima Estate orthodox tea came second at the Gold Medal Tea Awards of the North American Tea Conference in September 2025. “The success of our Orthodox tea expansion at Kitumbe gave us the confidence to accelerate this journey at Changana,” said Director and Chief Executive Officer Mr Dushanth Ratwatte.

Sources: Food Business MEA, 5 Nov · EATTA auction statistics

Tea culture

Lanterns and tea-tribe games in Assam. The sixth Folk Tea Festival takes place on 28 and 29 November at Hatipoti Tea Estate in Pabhoi, Biswanath Chariali, in India’s Assam, The Sentinel reported. The first evening ends with a mass sky lantern release, folk music from the Hirak Jyoti Sharma Folk Ensemble, a play directed by Nayan Prasad and a concert by singer Maitreyee Patar. On the second day, the estate hosts Indigenous Tea-Tribe Games, meant to keep the old pastimes of Assam’s tea communities alive, before Tanmoy Saikia closes the festival. Visitors can camp on the estate and book tea-tasting sessions. Mashamba ya chai si kazi tu: in Assam, the tea garden is also a place to sing and play.

Source: The Sentinel, 10 Nov

One number
