About Chai Weekly

The world of tea, one story at a time

Independent coverage of the global tea industry, its markets, people and culture. Published from East Africa, with a global outlook. Free to read, every Wednesday.

Our mission

Tea is one of East Africa’s great exports and one of the world’s most loved drinks, yet the people who grow, make and trade it rarely get a publication written for them. Chai Weekly sets out to change that: to explain the week in tea clearly and accurately, from the price a factory’s tea fetched at Mombasa on Monday to the rules that decide whether it can be sold in Europe next year.

An East African view of a global industry

We look at tea from East Africa. That means the ten countries whose tea is traded through the East African Tea Trade Association and the Mombasa auction: Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Burundi, Ethiopia, the DR Congo, Malawi, Madagascar and Mozambique. Every edition has an East Africa section. From there we follow the rest of the tea world, from Colombo, Kolkata and Chittagong to the packers and drinkers of Pakistan, Egypt, the United Kingdom and beyond. Auction prices are given in US dollars, as the auctions publish them, because our readers include producers well beyond Kenya.

Who we write for

Tea farmers and factory staff, brokers, buyers and packers, researchers, students and policymakers, and anyone who simply loves a good cup. We write in plain English, short and clear, for readers who often read on a phone.

Independent and free

Chai Weekly is independent. No tea board, company, broker or political party owns it or approves what it publishes. It is free to read and always will be. When a sponsor supports the magazine, their message is clearly labelled “Sponsored”, is kept apart from the reporting, and has no say over what we write.

How we work

  • Every figure has a source. Prices, volumes and payments link to the report they came from, and say which sale or date they refer to.
  • Market data is never estimated. If an auction has not published a price, we say so rather than guess. Mombasa figures come from EATTA and are marked provisional until EATTA finalises them on Friday.
  • We check before we publish. Stories about appointments and court cases are checked against later reports from more than one outlet.
  • People get their proper titles. We write “President William Ruto”, then “President Ruto”, and give every official the same courtesy.
  • Readers’ stories are real. From the shamba carries only stories and photos that readers send us, published with their permission.
  • We correct mistakes openly. If we get something wrong, tell us and we will fix it and say so.

What’s in each edition

The Mombasa auction in plain numbers and the week’s other auctions; KTDA and factory news; East Africa; the wider tea world; the weather in the tea areas; a practical tip for the shamba; a reader’s story; a factory spotlight; tea culture; open tenders and jobs; and the week ahead.
