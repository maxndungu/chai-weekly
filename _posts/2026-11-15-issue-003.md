NAIROBI — Tea at the Mombasa auction sold for its best price of the year last week, as tight supply after a dry spell met steady demand from Pakistan and the Middle East. At KTDA, a new acting chief executive took office in the middle of a court fight over who chairs the board. In Kampala, MPs began work on a law to make Uganda sell less of its tea raw. And the rains that held off for weeks are now falling across the western tea counties.

## The auction this week

The average price at Sale 45 on 9 November was **US$2.36 a kilo for CTC tea**, up 3 cents from Sale 44 and the highest of 2026, according to EATTA. It was the second rise in a row, and 14 cents above Sale 40 in early October. Buyers took **6.84 million kg**, slightly more than the week before.

- **Unsold tea:** 15.26% of the 7.5 million kg offered went unsold, according to brokers Forbes & Walker.
- **Who was buying:** Pakistani packers, Yemen and other Middle Eastern buyers "maintained support", Forbes & Walker said.
- **Orthodox tea:** the last orthodox average was $3.58 a kilo, at Sale 44.
- **The year so far:** Mombasa has averaged $2.21 a kilo across 45 sales in 2026, with a low of $2.09 in May. See every sale on our [Auction analysis]({{ '/auctions/' | relative_url }}) page.

**Why prices are rising:** there is less tea. KTDA factories received 6.3% less green leaf in July to September than a year earlier, The Standard reported, and premium teas gained as supply tightened.

## KTDA and factory news

**Kanja Thuku is KTDA's acting group CEO.** The KTDA Holding board appointed Mr Thuku from 10 November, replacing Eng Francis Miano, who is retiring. Mr Thuku has worked at KTDA for 34 years and was Group Director, Special Programmes. KTDA Holding Board chairman Enos Njeru announced the move and said the board wants the acting CEO to focus on "stabilising earnings for smallholder tea factories". Eng Miano had held the job in an acting capacity since January, after the exit of former group CEO Wilson Muthaura.

**The chair is contested.** KTDA named James Githinji Mwangi as chairman after a board meeting on 3 November. Mr Njeru challenged that meeting, saying six board members were not told about it, and a court order has reinstated him. KTDA placed a notice in the national newspapers saying Mr Njeru remains the substantive chairman while the conservatory orders stand.

**Less leaf, smaller cheques.** Deliveries from July to September were 212 million kg, against 225 million kg a year earlier. Kiambu fell furthest (21.8%), followed by Kisii (10.4%), lower Murang'a (10.3%) and Meru (9.2%). Kirinyaga barely changed.

**What it means for farmers:** prices at the auction are improving, but income follows kilos as well as price. The leadership fight matters too: the board's decisions on costs and payments set what reaches your account.

## East Africa

**Uganda drafts a law to sell less tea raw.** Uganda's Parliament on 28 October gave Hon. Cohen Amanya, MP for Igara County West, leave to bring a National Tea Bill. It would require buyers and exporters to blend, grade, pack or brand tea before export, under a certificate from the agriculture ministry. Exporters who skip that step could lose their licence and pay a fine equal to the value of the tea. The Bill also proposes a domestic auction, licences across the value chain and regular inspection of farms and factories.

About 90% of Uganda's tea is sold through the Mombasa auction, and lower grades fetch under US$1 a kilo, Hon. Amanya told the House. Hon. Methuselah Kasukaali, MP for Mwenge Central, said Uganda earned US$54 million from tea in 2024 and could reach US$120 million with better quality. State Minister for Agriculture Hon. Desire Muhooza said the ministry is still consulting the industry.

**Growers want more than an auction.** Tea consultant and farmer Mr Onesimus Matsiko told New Vision that Uganda must first fix leaf quality and yields, because "an auction alone" cannot solve those problems. Farmer Mr Dick Dunstan Turinawe called a proposed Sh100 million penalty "anti-people" and asked for formal consultation with outgrowers, the Uganda Tea Association and EATTA.

**Why it matters for Kenya:** Ugandan tea is one of the biggest foreign offerings at Mombasa. If Uganda keeps more tea at home to blend or sell at its own auction, there would be less of it at Mombasa, which could firm prices for Kenyan teas. Brokers and buyers will be watching how fast the Bill moves.

## Around the tea world

**India: a floor price for tea.** A 10-member committee set up by the Tea Board of India has recommended a minimum sustainable price for tea, linked to wages and consumer prices and reviewed regularly. Five industry bodies, including the Indian Tea Association and UPASI, want the Ministry of Commerce to adopt it quickly. Tea prices have grown about 2 to 3% a year while input costs grew 10 to 14%, and small growers produce about 55% of India's tea. "The tea industry is confronted with mounting cost pressures," said Mr C K Dhanuka, chairman of Dhunseri Tea and Industries.

**Sri Lanka: best Westerns slip.** At Colombo's Sale 44 on 10 and 11 November, 5.44 million kg was offered. Best Western BOP and BOPF grades fell by about Rs20 a kilo and Nuwara Eliya teas met poor demand, Forbes & Walker said. Shippers to the UK and Europe stayed quiet while buyers for the CIS were more active. The previous sale averaged $3.68 a kilo.

**Why it matters for Kenya:** India is a direct competitor in CTC tea. A legal price floor there would lift the cost of Indian tea for buyers, which could help demand for Kenyan tea at Mombasa.

## Weather in the tea counties

Kenya Met's five-day outlook from 14 November expects **heavier showers and thunderstorms** over the Rift Valley and Lake Victoria basin, mainly in the afternoon and night. That includes Kericho, Bomet, Nandi, Kisii and Nyamira. East of the Rift, Nyeri, Kirinyaga, Murang'a, Kiambu, Meru and Embu expect **moderate rain** from mid-morning, heavier later in the day, and **nights there may drop to 5°C.**

## Shamba tip

**When the flush comes back:** after weeks of slow growth, rain brings a burst of new leaf.

1. **Keep plucking rounds tight**, about every 7 to 10 days, so shoots don't grow past two leaves and a bud. Coarse leaf lowers the price per kilo.
2. **Clear drains and paths before the storms** on sloping plots.
3. **Deliver leaf the same day.** Wet leaf heats up in a bag.

Your factory's extension officer can advise on the right round for your area.

## Tea culture

**A tea trail race in Nepal.** Ilam, in the hills of eastern Nepal, holds its first Suryodaya Tea Festival on 21 and 22 November. It has a 12.5 km men's race and a 9 km women's race through the tea gardens, plus tea tasting, garden treks, food stalls and cultural processions. The organisers expect about 10,000 visitors and are building a 25.5 km tea trail from Kanyam. 

*Source: [The Rising Nepal, 7 Nov](https://risingnepaldaily.com/news/88501)*
