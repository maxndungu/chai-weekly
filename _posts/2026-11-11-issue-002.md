NAIROBI — Kenyan tea is holding its price but there is less of it to sell. KTDA factories put 11% less tea through the Mombasa auction last week, and their chairman blames the late arrival of the short rains. Two pieces of policy news could help: Sudan has lifted its 20-month ban on Kenyan tea, and the High Court has suspended the contested tea levy.

## The auction this week

KTDA-managed factories earned **KSh968.68 million from 2.51 million kg** at Monday's Mombasa auction, down from KSh1.09 billion on 2.84 million kg the week before, according to EATTA figures reported by The Standard and Sacco Review. The fall came from volume, not price.

- **Kenyan average: KSh318.63/kg (US$2.47).** That is level with the previous week, and up from $2.36 in mid-October.
- **Top prices (Mt Kenya):** Githongo KSh442 ($3.43), Mununga KSh439, Kinoro and Gacharage KSh437, Imenti KSh436.
- **Lowest in Mt Kenya:** Michimikuru KSh363.78 ($2.82) and Kanyenya-ini KSh380.55. Michimikuru topped the whole auction in October.
- **Neighbours:** Rwanda fetched $3.42/kg, Burundi $1.53 and Uganda $1.33.
- **Buyers:** 43 companies took part. LAB International bought the most at 1.01 million kg, followed by Global Tea (865,127 kg) and Mombasa Coffee (574,346 kg).

## KTDA and factory news

**Sudan lifts its ban on Kenyan tea.** Sudan has reopened its market to Kenyan tea, ending a ban imposed in March 2025, President William Ruto announced on 11 November. The ban followed a diplomatic rift after the Sudanese RSF militia and allied groups signed a political charter in Nairobi. The decision came after President Ruto met Sudan's Prime Minister Kamil Idris at the COMESA summit in Harare. According to the Tea Board of Kenya, exports to Sudan fell from 20 million kg to 8.7 million kg during the dispute, and earnings fell from KSh4.5 billion to KSh1.93 billion. No date has been given for shipments to resume.

**The tea levy is on hold.** Sacco Review reported on 5 November that the High Court had suspended the contested tea levy a week earlier. Buyers have blamed the levy all year for weak prices, and the Tea Buyers Association put its cost at about KSh80,000 per container. Prices rose at the first auction after the ruling, and KTDA factories earned KSh1.09 billion. The case is not over, so watch for the next hearing date. Githunguri MP Gathoni Wamuchomba has also called for the 0.8% levy to be suspended.

**Farmers are pushing back on bonuses.** Farmers in Litein protested a bonus of KSh18 per kg. Farmers at Thumaita factory are demanding a refund of KSh117 million that was deducted for fertiliser. Factory bonuses this year ranged from KSh12.50 to KSh50 per kg.

**What it means for farmers:** prices per kilo are holding up, but this year's smaller bonuses come from smaller crops. Until the leaf comes back, monthly payments will stay tight.

## Around the tea world

**Sri Lanka: October average up, buyers selective.** Colombo's national auction average for October was Rs1,217.46/kg (US$3.67), up from Rs1,202.74 in September, according to brokers Forbes & Walker. At the 3–4 November sale, 5.32 million kg was offered. Demand was "fair", good low-grown teas stayed firm, and Nuwara Eliya had what the brokers called "a disappointing sale". Shippers to the UK and Europe were barely active, while buyers for Japan, China, the CIS and the Middle East kept bidding.

**Bangladesh: Chittagong cools as the sale goes on.** At Sale 28 on 9 November, 2.91 million kg was offered and 85% sold, down from 89% the week before. National Brokers said the market "opened with a fairly good note" but eased as the sale went on. Loose-tea buyers held back, while dusts sold well.

**India: demand is back after the festivals.** North Indian auctions resumed after the festival break, and better-liquoring CTC teas drew higher bids, according to the Tea Price Report.

**Why it matters for Kenya:** buyers are picky everywhere right now. They pay for good teas and walk away from plain ones. That is why Kenya's best Mt Kenya factories earn KSh440 while the national average sits near KSh320.

## Weather in the tea counties

KTDA chairman Enos Njeru said this week that factories are "operating below capacity" because the rains came late. That fits poorly with the Met department's season outlook, which forecast above-average October to December rain for every tea county. We could not open KMD's November forecast this week, so check the [KMD monthly forecast page](https://meteo.go.ke/our-products/monthly-forecast/) for your county. Next week we'll report how much rain has actually fallen.

## Shamba tip

**When the rains run late:**

1. **Mulch young plants** with prunings or grass to hold moisture until the rains settle.
2. **Hold back fertiliser** until the soil is properly moist. Fertiliser on dry soil is wasted, and so is fertiliser washed off in a sudden storm.
3. **Pluck to standard.** With less leaf, it is tempting to pluck coarse leaf, but that is what drags down the price per kilo.

Your factory's extension officer can advise on timing for your area.

## Tea culture

**A festival in an Assam tea garden.** The 6th Folk Tea Festival takes place on 28–29 November at Hatipoti Tea Estate in Biswanath Chariali, Assam. It has sky lanterns, indigenous tea-tribe games, tea-tasting labs, tribal food pavilions and a car rally toward the Arunachal border. Kenya has nothing like it yet. Kericho, with its estates and its tea history, would be a fine place to start one.

## One number

**11%**: how much less tea KTDA factories sold at Mombasa this week than the week before. The price per kilo barely moved. What is missing is leaf, and that is why this year's bonuses are small.
