NAIROBI — More tea reached the Mombasa auction this week, and the price barely moved. Sale 46 on Monday sold 7.51 million kg, about 10% more than the week before, at an average of $2.35 a kilo, according to EATTA. KTDA's factories earned Sh1.47 billion, a third more than last week. Sudan's order lifting its 20-month ban on Kenyan tea is now in force, and in Europe tighter pesticide limits are squeezing one of Kenya's biggest rivals, Assam.

## The auction this week

The average price at **Sale 46 on 16 November was US$2.35 a kilo**, 1 cent below Sale 45, according to EATTA. CTC tea averaged **$2.34**, down 2 cents, and orthodox tea **$3.45**. Buyers took **7.51 million kg**, up from 6.84 million kg the week before.

- **Unsold tea:** 16.4% of the 8.2 million kg offered was not sold, according to Africa Tea Brokers' report carried by Forbes & Walker. Two-thirds of the small orthodox offering went unsold.
- **Who was buying:** demand was "good general", the brokers said. Pakistani packers gave more support, Egypt, Yemen and other Middle East buyers held steady, and Afghanistan, the UK, Kazakhstan and Russia were more active.
- **Kenyan tea:** averaged Ksh319.92 ($2.48) a kilo, up a cent on last week, Sacco Review reported from EATTA figures.
- **Best prices (east of the Rift):** Mununga $3.38 (Ksh436.02), Ngere and Imenti $3.31, Rukuriri $3.29. Factories east of the Rift fetched above $3.00 in the second week since the High Court suspended the tea levy, EATTA figures show.
- **Buyers:** 50 companies took part. Global Tea bought the most at 1.15 million kg, followed by LAB International (1.02 million kg) and Mitchell Cotts (777,073 kg), EATTA Managing Director Mr George Omuga said.
- **The year so far:** Mombasa has averaged $2.21 a kilo across 46 sales in 2026, with a low of $2.09 in May. Every sale is on our [Auction analysis]({{ '/auctions/' | relative_url }}) page.

**Why the price held:** demand was broad. The brokers reported more bidding from Pakistan, Afghanistan, the UK and the CIS, so most of the extra tea found buyers. Best dusts gained up to 54 US cents a kilo on better liquoring, the brokers said.

## KTDA and factory news

**Sh1.47 billion in a week.** KTDA-managed factories earned **Sh1.47 billion from 3.16 million kg** of made tea at Monday's sale, up from Sh1.09 billion on 2.84 million kg the week before, The Standard and Sacco Review reported. Kimunye sold the most (106,548 kg), followed by Rukuriri and Mungania. KTDA Holdings chairman Mr Enos Njeru praised growers in Embu County for the quality of their leaf.

**Sudan's door is formally open.** Sudan's Minister of Industry and Trade, Mahasin Ali Yaqoub, signed Ministerial Order No. 25 of 2026 lifting the ban on Kenyan tea on 8 November, with immediate effect, HapaKenya reported. The Tea Board of Kenya, in a statement signed by Chief Executive Officer Mr Willy Mutai, urged exporters to resume shipments at once while following export, quality and customs rules. According to the Board, exports to Sudan fell from about 20 million kg to 8.7 million kg during the 20-month ban. The order sets no quotas and no date for the first shipment.

**Paid by the fifth.** Mr Njeru said monthly green leaf payments have risen from Sh16 to Sh30 a kilo and now arrive on the 5th of each month, which he said has made growers more creditworthy. "In the past, farmers had to wait for an entire month before they accessed their money," he said in Runyenjes, Embu, where he has declared for the parliamentary seat, The Standard reported.

**Directors' bills under scrutiny.** The Standard reported that some factory boards spend Sh19 million to Sh30 million a year on six directors. At Mataara in Thika, directors' fees, expenses and tours passed Sh24 million in 2025 while turnover fell to Sh1.2 billion from Sh1.6 billion. Chinga in Nyeri spent Sh30.6 million on directors as turnover slipped to Sh2.29 billion.

**The chair goes back to court.** The High Court has set 24 November for the case over KTDA's chairmanship. Justice Francis Kyambia suspended the 3 November election of Mr James Githinji Mwangi and told Mr Njeru to keep acting as chairman until then, Sacco Review and Food Business MEA reported.

**The CEO's job is contested too.** The KTDA board named Mr Kanja Thuku, Group Director of Special Programmes, as acting group CEO from 10 November, saying Eng Francis Miano was proceeding on retirement, The Standard reported. A day later a court reinstated Eng Miano, the Daily Nation reported. He has been acting CEO since January, after the exit of former group CEO Mr Wilson Muthaura.

**What it means for farmers:** bigger volumes at steady prices mean more money in the factory accounts this month. How much reaches you still depends on each board's costs, so ask about them at your factory's next meeting.

## East Africa

**Rwanda: the best price at Mombasa again.** Rwandan tea averaged **$3.49 a kilo** (Ksh450.21) at Monday's sale, the highest of any origin, according to EATTA figures reported by Sacco Review. Rwanda sold 191,336 kg. Burundi's small offering of 2,380 kg averaged $2.52.

**Uganda: more tea, low prices.** Uganda sold 1.03 million kg at Mombasa, the biggest foreign offering, at an average of **$1.33 a kilo**, the same as in our last issue.

**Uganda: Igara Tea Factory wins four months.** The government has secured a four-month suspension of DFCU Bank's planned auction of Igara Tea Factory in Bushenyi over a loan of about Shs5 billion, Deputy Speaker of Parliament Thomas Tayebwa said. The factory stopped operating about seven months ago, leaving workers and farmers unpaid, Nile Post reported. Earlier, more than 7,000 smallholders met in Bushenyi and resolved to petition President Yoweri Museveni to stop the sale, The Cooperator reported. "The government is fully aware of these challenges and is working to address them," Deputy Speaker Tayebwa said.

**Why it matters for Kenya:** Ugandan tea is the largest foreign offering at Mombasa. A factory that has stopped means less leaf processed in western Uganda and unpaid growers across five districts.

## Around the tea world

**India: Europe's tighter pesticide limits squeeze Assam.** Since 7 March 2026, tea sold in the European Union must meet much lower maximum residue limits (MRLs) for two neonicotinoid insecticides, thiamethoxam and clothianidin, under EU Regulation 2023/334. A third, thiacloprid, was cut to the 0.05 ppm default in May 2025. This is not a ban: growers can still use the chemicals, but tea with residues above the limit cannot be sold in the EU. The limits arrive just as India's free trade deal with the EU, concluded on 27 January, removes duties on packed and flavoured teas. "The issues Indian teas face are not duty barriers but MRL compliance," Mr Mohit Agarwal, Director of Asian Tea Company, told STiR. Industry estimates quoted by STiR say high-end Assam exports to Europe could shrink by 60 to 70% if gardens fail to adapt.

Growers say the main alternatives are not yet approved for tea in India, KNN India reported, and Assam lacks testing capacity, especially for its small growers, who grow about half the state's crop, according to The Sentinel. About 40 million kg of Assam tea a year goes to the EU and UK, the Assam Tribune reported. "The EU notification on lowering MRL for three compounds has become critical," Tea Research Association secretary Mr Joydeep Phukan said. Britain sets its own limits since Brexit, and we could not confirm whether it has made the same cuts.

**Sri Lanka: prices up, a budget for smallholders.** Colombo's national average rose to $3.73 a kilo (Rs1,222.23) for the sale week ending 11 November, from $3.68, according to Forbes & Walker. At the 16–17 November sale, best Western teas gained Rs20 a kilo or more, while Nuwara Eliya teas stayed weak. The 2027 budget presented on 12 November by President Anura Kumara Dissanayake keeps a fertiliser subsidy of Rs4,000 a bag for smallholders.

**Bangladesh: Chittagong firms.** The average at Chittagong's Sale 29 on 16 November rose to Tk276.15 from Tk273.66, and 88.7% of the tea sold, National Brokers said.

**Why it matters for Kenya:** Kenyan and Assam teas compete for the same European blenders, and Kenyan exporters face the same EU limits. If Assam struggles to comply, buyers will look for clean, tested tea elsewhere.

## Shamba tip

**Make the most of the flush.** After a wet week, new leaf grows fast.

1. **Keep plucking rounds tight** so shoots don't grow past two leaves and a bud. Coarse leaf pulls down the price per kilo, and this week the best-plucked teas earned more than $3.
2. **Deliver leaf the same day.** Wet leaf packed tight in a bag heats up and spoils.
3. **Check drains on slopes** before the next heavy storm.

Your factory's extension officer can advise on the right round for your area.

## Factory spotlight

**Kapchorua Tea Kenya.** One of the few tea companies listed on the Nairobi Securities Exchange, Kapchorua had a strong half-year. In the six months to September 2026, revenue rose 12.4% to **Ksh932.7 million** and net profit rose 37.9% to **Ksh131.2 million**, Food Business MEA reported. Operating profit was up 77% and the operating margin widened to 14.3% from 9.1%. The company declared a dividend of Ksh30 a share for the year to March.

Kapchorua is an associate of Williamson Tea Kenya, which grows and processes tea in the western highlands. Williamson swung back to profit in the same half-year, with net profit of about Ksh284 million against a loss a year earlier, as revenue rose 24.8% to Ksh2.11 billion. Food Business MEA reported that the company benefited from better market conditions "despite prolonged dry weather earlier in the year."

## Tea culture

**The pickers who keep Mt Kenya's tea moving.** Tea farms east of the Rift, from Kiambu and Murang'a to Nyeri, Kirinyaga, Embu and Meru, now depend on pickers from western Kenya and, more recently, from Uganda, Rwanda and Burundi, The Standard reported. Pickers earn up to Sh17 a kilo in Meru and as little as Sh13 in parts of Kiambu and Murang'a. Growers who pay too little lose their workers to neighbours. "It is a hide-and-seek game between growers. You pay low, you suffer the shortages," said Mr Willy Mbugua of Mataara factory. Plucking machines are one answer, but they cost over Sh15,000, said Ms Anne Nyathira of Ndarugu factory. Kazi ya mikono bado inahitajika: the hands that pick Kenya's tea are now East African.

*Source: [The Standard, 11 Nov](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/national/article/2001562053/how-east-african-tea-picking-labour-rescued-mt-kenya)*
