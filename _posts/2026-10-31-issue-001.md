NAIROBI — The short rains have returned to Kenya's tea country, and with them a slow recovery at the Mombasa auction. After a mid-year slump, Kenyan tea is fetching more per kilo than it did in June, not because demand has surged but because there is simply less of it: bushes pruned in July are only now coming back into full plucking.

## The auction this week

The most recent Mombasa auction figures we could confirm (EATTA report, published 15 October) show prices slowly recovering from their mid-year slump:

- **Kenyan average: US$2.36/kg (about KSh304).** That is up from $2.23 in mid-June and $2.34 in late September.
- **Best price: Michimikuru (Meru) at KSh444.43/kg.** Most Mt Kenya factories sold above KSh387.
- **Best west of the Rift: Momul at KSh347.01/kg.**
- **Volume: 6.5 million kg** taken by 47 buyers. KTDA factories sold 2.8 million kg for KSh1.04 billion, up from 2.6 million kg (KSh950 million) the week before.
- **Rwandan tea is still out-pricing ours**, at $3.22/kg.

**Why prices are firming:** supply is tight. Many farmers pruned their bushes after July on extension advice and are only now coming back into full plucking, according to KTDA chairman Enos Njeru.

**What's still dragging:** buyers keep pointing at the export levy introduced in May (the Tea Buyers Association puts it at roughly KSh80,000 per container) and at shipping disruption through the Middle East. Earlier in the year, unsold tea at the auction hit 27% of what was offered in a single sale.

## KTDA and factory news

**September pay and bonuses landed on 12 October.** KTDA released the September monthly payment together with the 2025/26 second payment (the "bonus") on 9 October, and farmers could draw it from Monday the 12th. Bonuses ranged from **KSh12/kg at the lowest-paying factory to KSh50/kg at the highest**, and the monthly average was about KSh30/kg of green leaf. Several Mt Kenya factories paid less than last year.

**Why bonuses fell even as prices recovered:** less leaf. Green leaf delivered in the first 11 months of 2025/26 was 1.095 billion kg, down from 1.144 billion kg the year before. The government also put KSh850 million into 17 KTDA-managed factories.

**The target to watch:** Agriculture CS Mutahi Kagwe wants green leaf paying KSh100/kg by 2027. At today's numbers that is a long way off.

## Around the tea world

**Sri Lanka: the smallest Colombo auction in over two years.** Only 4.83 million kg was offered at the 22–23 September sale, the lowest since April 2024. Tight supply has kept prices steady, and August export earnings rose to Rs1,910/kg (about US$5.74) even as volumes slipped. Türkiye has become Sri Lanka's biggest buyer, taking 143% more tea than a year ago, while Iraq bought 37% less. Middle East shipping trouble has hit Colombo too, just as it has Mombasa.

**Japan: matcha demand is outrunning the farms.** Japan's matcha exports hit a record ¥72.1 billion in 2025, double the year before, and January to August 2026 exports are running at about 1.8 times last year. But Japan's main tea-growing area has shrunk to 25,400 hectares, around 40% of its bushes are over 30 years old, and farmers are ageing without successors. Kagoshima is building a ¥2.6 billion matcha factory to lift capacity from 10 to 300 tonnes a year, opening in December 2027.

**India: Assam auctions for flood relief.** A special charity sale at the Guwahati Tea Auction Centre raised ₹2.26 crore for families hit by this year's Assam floods. Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma ran the hammer himself, and his 12 ceremonial lots brought in ₹99.5 lakh.

**Why it matters for Kenya:** our main competitors are dealing with the same thing we are. Supply is tight and shipping is disrupted. Meanwhile the money is moving toward speciality teas like matcha, and that is a market Kenyan purple and green tea growers are trying to break into.

## Weather in the tea counties

The Kenya Meteorological Department's October to December outlook is good news for the bushes, with a few warnings attached.

- **Every major tea county is forecast above-average rain** (more than 125% of normal): Kericho, Bomet, Nandi, Kisii and Nyamira in the west, and Nyeri, Murang'a, Kirinyaga, Kiambu, Embu and Meru east of the Rift.
- **West of the Rift**, the rains simply carry on from September and are expected to continue into January.
- **East of the Rift**, onset was forecast for the second to third week of October, also lasting into January.
- **A strong El Niño** is running, which tends to boost short-rains totals.
- **Watch for:** heavy downpours, landslides on steep slopes, soil erosion and nutrient leaching, and more pests and disease in the humid weather.

*Source: [KMD OND 2026 Seasonal Forecast (PDF)](https://meteo.go.ke/documents/4790/OND_2026__Seasonal_Forecast.pdf)*

## Shamba tip: fertilizer and wet weather

KMD's own advice this season is that fertilizer applied **after** the rains start is likely to be washed away. For tea that means:

1. **Don't top-dress into a downpour.** Wait for a dry window of a few days, and apply when the soil is moist but not running.
2. **Split the dose** into smaller applications rather than one big one, so less is lost to leaching.
3. **Keep drains and contour bunds clear** on sloping plots before the heaviest weeks. Erosion takes topsoil and fertilizer together.
4. **Plan infilling now.** KMD lists perennial crops like tea as a "positive opportunity" this season, so it is a good window to fill gaps in your rows.

Your factory's extension officer will know the right rates for your soil and the KTDA fertilizer schedule.

## Tea culture: the chai that isn't "tea"

Kenya is one of the world's biggest black tea exporters, but at home most of us don't drink "tea" in the British sense. We drink **chai**, where the tea is boiled *in* the milk.

**Chai ya tangawizi (ginger chai), for 2 cups**

- 1 cup water, 1 cup milk
- 2 heaped teaspoons Kenyan CTC tea (any supermarket packet works; CTC is the small granules)
- A thumb of fresh ginger, crushed
- Sugar to taste

Boil the water with the ginger for two minutes. Add the tea and let it boil for a minute until it's dark. Pour in the milk, bring it back up until it rises (watch it), then lower the heat and simmer one more minute. Strain, sweeten, and drink with mandazi.

**Try it:** add a crushed cardamom pod or two with the ginger and you're halfway to *chai masala*.
