NAIROBI — President William Ruto has promised KSh3.7 billion to modernise Kenya's tea factories, after leaders from Gusii complained that their farmers are paid far less than growers east of the Rift. A parliamentary inquiry found that East Rift tea sold for an average of US$2.95 a kilo in 2024/25, against US$1.78 for West Rift tea, even though the West grows most of the country's crop. Separately, Sudan has published the order that formally ends its 20-month ban on Kenyan tea.

## The auction this week

There has been no new Mombasa sale since our last issue. The most recent, on 9 November, put Kenyan tea at **KSh318.63/kg (US$2.47)**, level with the week before, with Mt Kenya's Githongo on top at KSh442, according to EATTA figures reported by The Standard.

For the wider market, John Keells' latest report gives the Mombasa average across all teas at Sale 44 (2 November) as **US$2.33/kg**, up from US$2.28 at Sale 43. That figure includes teas from neighbouring countries, which is why it sits below the Kenyan-origin average.

## KTDA and factory news

**KSh3.7 billion for tea factories.** Speaking in Nyamira County, President Ruto announced KSh3.7 billion (about US$28.5 million) to modernise tea factories, Food Business Middle East & Africa reported on 10 November. KSh1.2 billion has already been released and KSh2.5 billion is set aside for the next financial year. The promise followed complaints from Gusii leaders about low payments. Ruto pointed to ageing machinery, inefficiency and high electricity costs in the West Rift.

**Why the West earns less.** A National Assembly agriculture committee inquiry found that East Rift tea averaged US$2.95/kg at auction in 2024/25, against US$1.78/kg for West Rift tea. The West Rift produces about 70% of Kenya's tea. This year East Rift farmers, including Mt Kenya, received bonuses of up to KSh60 per kg, while Gusii and West Rift payments were much lower. The committee called for new factory machinery, audits of factory hydropower projects, lifestyle audits of factory managers and more work on export markets.

**Sudan's ban is formally over.** Sudan's Ministry of Industry and Trade issued Ministerial Order No. 25 of 2026 on 8 November, lifting the ban on Kenyan tea with immediate effect, according to Radio Tamazuj. Sudan once bought about 35 million kg of Kenyan tea a year, worth around US$70 million. When the ban began in March 2025, 207 containers were left stranded at Mombasa. The order does not say when shipments will resume.

**What it means for farmers:** the factory money is a promise, spread over two years, and it is not yet clear which factories will get it. If you deliver to a West Rift factory, ask your directors how they plan to bid for it.

*Sources: [Food Business MEA, 10 Nov](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/kenya-allocates-us28-5m-to-modernise-tea-factories-amid-low-farmer-bonuses/) · [Radio Tamazuj, 11 Nov](https://www.radiotamazuj.org/en/news/article/sudan-lifts-ban-on-kenyan-tea-imports-after-20-months)*

## Around the tea world

**India: industry asks for a minimum price.** Five Indian tea associations, including the Indian Tea Association and the small growers' body CISTA, on 10 November urged the commerce ministry to adopt a Tea Board committee's proposal for a "minimum sustainable price", Business Standard reported. The floor would be linked to an index of wages and consumer prices. Labour makes up about 60% of production costs, and the Sentinel reported that tea prices have grown 2–3% a year against 10–14% for costs. No rupee figure has been set.

**India: a big September crop.** India produced 184.22 million kg of tea in September, up 14.71% on September 2025, according to Tea Board of India estimates reported by the Sentinel. Assam grew 110.90 million kg of it, about 60%.

**Sri Lanka: more tea, choosy buyers.** Colombo offered 5.44 million kg at Sale 44 on 10–11 November, slightly more than the week before, according to Forbes & Walker. Demand was fair but prices were irregular: the best Western BOP/BOPFs fell about Rs20/kg and Nuwara Eliya teas met poor demand.

**Why it matters for Kenya:** a large Indian crop means more CTC competing for the same buyers. And an Indian price floor, if it comes, would be watched closely by Kenyan growers asking for the same.

## Shamba tip

**The cheapest way to a better price.** This week's numbers show how much leaf quality matters: the factories with the best prices are the ones whose leaf is consistently fine.

1. **Two leaves and a bud.** Coarse leaf adds weight today but lowers what your factory earns per kilo for everyone.
2. **Keep to your plucking round** so shoots do not run coarse between visits.
3. **Keep leaf cool and loose** in the basket, out of the sun, and get it to the buying centre quickly.

Majani mazuri, bei nzuri. Your factory's extension officer can advise on plucking rounds for your area.

## Tea culture

**Ceylon tea goes to dinner.** On 10 November the Sri Lanka Tea Board hosted diplomats, officials and industry guests at Temple Trees in Colombo for an evening called "Beyond the cup", attended by Prime Minister Harini Amarasuriya. The aim was to show off the heritage and range of Ceylon tea to the world. Kericho could host an evening like it.
